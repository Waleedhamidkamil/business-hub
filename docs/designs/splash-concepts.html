The gate

My pick

Your idea. Two doors meet in the middle and fill the app window. The brand is on the left door. The right door, where a login form would be, shows the app signing itself in. When the dashboard is ready, the doors part in about 0.6 seconds and the real dashboard is already sitting behind them.

Right door
Seal on the seam
  • Welcome: your name, the date, and four checks that tick off as each real stage finishes. The last tick opens the gate.
  • Company: the "login" is picking the company. The last one you used is already selected and opens by itself. Click the other one while it loads to switch.
  • Brief: three facts from today's data while you wait.

Launch card

Simplest

A compact card in the centre of the screen, the way Office, Visual Studio and Adobe apps open. Company logo, the Business Hub name, one thin progress line with the stage written underneath, and the version and date at the bottom. When the dashboard is ready the card fades out and the app is already there behind it.

  • Small and calm. Nothing to look at for long, which is the point.
  • Shows which company is opening, so you never land in the wrong one.
  • Progress is written in words as well as drawn, so it never relies on colour.
  • The least "wow" of the three.

B

Seamless hand-off

The splash fills the whole app window in the app's own background colour. When the dashboard is ready, the logo glides into its place in the banner and the cards rise in around it. It feels like the app builds itself rather than replacing one screen with another, the way Android 12 and iPhone apps open.

  • The most polished moment; no "window pops in" feeling at all.
  • Matches light or dark, so there is never a white flash.
  • About twice the work: the glide needs the native window and the page to agree on exact positions.
  • The full-window cover hides the desktop while loading.

C

Morning brief

The useful one. The company data is open after only 0.2 seconds, long before the window is, so the splash can greet you and show three real facts while the rest loads: what needs attention, unpaid invoices, today's rate. You have read your day before the dashboard even appears.

  • Turns the waiting time into information.
  • Facts come from the same data the dashboard uses, read once.
  • On a fast launch the facts are on screen for about a second, so they must be short.
  • Repeats what the dashboard shows a moment later.

Appears in about 0.1 s

Drawn by Windows itself from a ready-made image, on its own thread, before the heavy app window starts. That is how Visual Studio does it, for the same reason. Building it inside the app page would leave the first 1.5 seconds blank.

Leaves the moment the app is ready

The dashboard sends one "ready" signal after its first real paint. No timer and no fake delay: 1 second launch, 1 second splash. A safety limit closes it anyway if something goes wrong, so it can never get stuck on screen.

Honest progress

The line moves through the real stages: company data, window, interface, dashboard. Between stages it eases forward but never reaches the next mark early and never goes backwards.

Knows the company and the theme

It reads the saved company and light or dark setting before anything else, so Eshraqat opens maroon, Abraj opens navy, and dark mode never flashes white.
