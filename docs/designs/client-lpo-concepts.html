Client LPOs - design proposal

Three ways to make the LPO screen the place you work from

Every mockup below uses your real Abraj LPOs as they stand today, 3 October 2026: 4 rentals, 1 supply contract, 3 materials orders, all HDEC. Nothing here is wired to the app. Statuses always carry a word and a shape, never colour alone.

What the screen does todayA long list of 8 rows with a pill each, then a detail page where the PDF takes two thirds of the space and ten buttons share the rest.
What it should answer first"What do I have to do on my LPOs today?" Right now that takes opening each one. Today the answer is 7 things, with $5,863.40 not yet billed plus 6 supply months nobody has counted.
Why the three types need different viewsA rental is billed month by month, a supply contract has no total at all, and a materials order moves delivered then invoiced. One generic row hides all three.

Best for
  • Working ON one LPO: billing, items and documents without page changes.
  • Ten buttons become one main action (the next thing to do) plus a "More" menu.
  • Jump between LPOs from the left list in one click.
Trade-off
  • The biggest rebuild of the three.
  • The PDF is no longer the first thing you see; it moves to the Documents tab.

My recommendation: combine them, in this order

Land on

Concept 1 · TodayThe "needs you" queue above the list. Each list row draws its own type's progress (month squares, delivered and invoiced steps).
→

Click an LPO

Concept 3 · WorkspaceList on the left, the LPO on the right, one main button, "More" menu, five tabs.
→

Inside the tabs

Concept 2 · Type viewsA rental's Billing tab is the timeline, the supply contract's is the month grid, a materials order's Items tab is the delivered and invoiced pipeline.

So nothing is lost: you open on what to do, work on one LPO without page changes, and every type gets the view that fits it. It can ship in steps: the queue first (smallest, most useful), then the workspace, then the type views.
