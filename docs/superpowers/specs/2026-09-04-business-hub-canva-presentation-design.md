# Business Hub Canva Presentation Design

## Objective

Create a premium, sales focused Business Hub presentation that also works as a professional product case study. The deck should persuade business buyers while demonstrating the quality of the product design and implementation.

The presentation will use the real Business Hub interface with isolated fictional content. Every slide will display the exact disclosure `DEMO DATA`.

## Audience

The primary audience includes business owners, procurement teams, and operations managers who manage quotations, purchase orders, delivery notes, invoices, costing, client records, and business documents.

The secondary audience includes employers, partners, and technical professionals reviewing the product as a design and engineering case study.

## Deliverables

1. One editable 16:9 Canva presentation.
2. One editable PowerPoint export when Canva export supports it without layout damage.
3. Twelve high resolution PNG slides for LinkedIn and portfolio use.
4. One PDF presentation for convenient sharing.
5. Updated presentation copy and accessible image descriptions.

The PNG target is 3840 by 2160 pixels when Canva export options permit. The minimum acceptable output is 1920 by 1080 pixels.

## Creative Direction

The approved direction is Premium Dark Technology.

The deck uses a deep navy canvas with cool white typography. Teal provides structure and amber highlights important numbers. Red appears only where the product itself identifies a loss or risk. Fine grid texture may appear in the background at low contrast.

The presentation should feel precise, composed, and technically credible. Screenshots remain the main evidence. Decorative elements must support hierarchy without competing with the interface.

### Color System

- Primary background: `#061525`
- Secondary background: `#0A2239`
- Main text: `#F5F8FB`
- Secondary text: `#A7BBCA`
- Structural accent: `#58E2C2`
- Numeric accent: `#FFB44C`
- Risk accent: use the product's existing red only for genuine loss or warning states

### Typography

Use a strong geometric sans serif for titles and a highly legible sans serif for supporting copy. The preferred pairing is Space Grotesk for titles and Inter for body text. If the selected Canva Brand Kit defines suitable fonts, use the Brand Kit fonts while preserving the approved hierarchy.

- Cover title: 46 to 58 pt
- Slide title: 34 to 42 pt
- Body copy: 19 to 24 pt
- Labels and proof points: 14 to 17 pt
- Visible disclosure: 13 to 15 pt

### Composition

The deck uses four layout families:

1. Cinematic cover with a large promise and one dominant product view.
2. Product proof slide with one headline, one short explanation, two proof points, and one readable screenshot.
3. Document gallery slide with large output pages and minimal labels.
4. Closing slide with a concise summary and invitation to discuss the product.

The repeated workflow strip from the first marketing cards will not appear on every slide. Workflow context will appear only where it improves understanding. Screenshots should use purposeful crops so the relevant interface remains readable at LinkedIn feed size.

## Writing Standard

The writing will be direct, specific, and commercial.

- No em dashes.
- No semicolons.
- No slogans without supporting evidence.
- No generic claims such as seamless, revolutionary, or game changing.
- No unsupported savings, speed, revenue, or productivity claims.
- Most slides use one headline and one supporting sentence.
- Titles name the product capability or state a claim that the screenshot supports.
- Feature lists use no more than two proof points unless the layout clearly benefits from a third.
- Every disclosure reads exactly `DEMO DATA`.

## Narrative and Slide Plan

### Slide 1: Commercial operations in one connected system

Goal: Introduce Business Hub and establish the full commercial scope.

Content: Business Hub keeps quotations, purchase orders, delivery notes, and invoices connected from first request to payment.

Visual: Cinematic dashboard crop with 180 demo documents and 14 demo clients shown as supporting context.

### Slide 2: One workflow replaces scattered document handling

Goal: Frame the operational problem and the product response.

Content: Commercial records, costing, follow up, and client history stay in one desktop workspace.

Visual: Clear QUT, PO, DN, and INV relationship using product evidence rather than a decorative process diagram.

### Slide 3: A live view of commercial work

Goal: Show the operational dashboard as the daily starting point.

Content: Search, document creation, attention items, and current business activity are visible from one screen.

Visual: Large dashboard crop with the attention queue and insights area readable.

### Slide 4: Margin visibility before a quote leaves

Goal: Demonstrate commercial control before submission.

Content: Each line shows sell price, remembered cost, currency, and profit. Losses appear in words and numbers.

Visual: Costing and Profit screen with the loss state clearly visible.

### Slide 5: A quotation workbench built for real item detail

Goal: Show the depth of the quotation editor.

Content: Bilingual descriptions, quantities, rates, pictures, costs, and approval state remain together while the quotation is prepared.

Visual: Readable quotation editor crop focused on item rows and commercial controls.

### Slide 6: Document relationships remain visible

Goal: Show continuity from quotation through delivery and invoicing.

Content: Linked references preserve the path between commercial documents and reduce the need to reconstruct history manually.

Visual: Invoice workbench or linked document view with the source relationship highlighted.

### Slide 7: Outstanding work becomes an explicit queue

Goal: Demonstrate operational follow up.

Content: Open bid decisions, unpaid invoices, delivery work, unsubmitted documents, and missing costs appear as named actions.

Visual: To Do screen with text labels and status shapes visible.

### Slide 8: Each client has a commercial history

Goal: Show the client profile as operational memory.

Content: Documents, totals, addresses, LPOs, and saved terms come together in one client record.

Visual: Client profile with summary and timeline areas.

### Slide 9: Master Data surfaces cleanup work

Goal: Demonstrate catalogue scale and data quality controls.

Content: Bilingual item records carry price, cost, unit, and picture status. Health filters expose gaps and possible duplicates.

Visual: Master Data list paired with a focused duplicate review inset.

### Slide 10: Client LPO acknowledgement stays controlled

Goal: Explain the signature and stamp workflow.

Content: The original client document stays untouched while signature and stamp placements are saved as a separate acknowledged copy.

Visual: LPO viewer with two visible placement marks and the save action.

### Slide 11: Consistent documents from dedicated PDF engines

Goal: Show output quality across the document set.

Content: Each document keeps its required fields and structure while sharing one recognizable visual system.

Visual: Quotation, purchase order, delivery note, and invoice pages at a readable scale.

### Slide 12: Separate companies, one desktop application

Goal: Close with workspace isolation and invite discussion.

Content: Each company keeps separate data, numbering, documents, and brand assets inside the same desktop application.

Visual: Profile switcher with a concise closing line and contact invitation.

## Canva Production Workflow

1. Connect the Canva plugin and list the available Brand Kits.
2. Use the only Brand Kit automatically, or ask the user to choose if several kits exist.
3. Generate presentation candidates from this specification.
4. Review the candidates against the approved premium dark direction.
5. Create the selected candidate as an editable Canva design.
6. Replace any generic generated interface imagery with the supplied real Business Hub screenshots.
7. Refine all text, crops, alignment, and spacing manually where needed.
8. Export the final deck to PNG, PDF, and PowerPoint when the export preserves the design.

## Accessibility and Evidence

Status must never depend on color alone. Use text labels, icons, borders, and shapes to reinforce meaning. Maintain strong contrast between the navy backgrounds and all text.

Product screenshots must come from the isolated showcase environment. No real client, supplier, address, bank account, signature, transaction, or company record may appear. Do not fabricate performance outcomes or customer endorsements.

## Quality Checks

Before delivery:

1. Inspect every slide at full size and at a reduced LinkedIn feed size.
2. Confirm all screenshots are sharp and intentionally cropped.
3. Confirm text does not overflow, overlap, or fall below the minimum sizes.
4. Confirm every slide displays exactly `DEMO DATA`.
5. Search all slide text for the em dash character and remove every occurrence.
6. Confirm all quantities and product facts match the isolated demo environment.
7. Confirm status labels remain understandable without color.
8. Confirm the PDF and PowerPoint exports match the approved Canva presentation.

## Scope Boundaries

This work changes only showcase and presentation assets. It does not modify the Business Hub application, live databases, live settings, document files, or customer information.
